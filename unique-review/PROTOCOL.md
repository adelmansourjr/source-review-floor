# Unique reviews over overlapping majority decisions

Frozen before evaluation. This study reuses the five pinned historical approval collections, the first 32 shuffled target ballots for calibration, the target's seven selected projects, and the equal-weight other-source prior from the parent study. It is a fresh observation design on the same historical ballots. The 35 triplet-majority decisions for a ballot share one noisy report per project, and a paid review of a ballot/project supplies one noisy answer reused wherever that project occurs. Restricted decoders use only the reports and reviews of the three projects in their target. There is no review-answer adaptation. Expected error integrates the binary review channel conditional on the true ballot and the observed report. Review costs count questions, with no measure of annotator time.

- For each source and tape sample seven independent project-report flip indicators per ballot at 24%, followed by one ballot-wide flip indicator at 12%. Tapes 0–5 use seeds 37925191 + 17 × source index + tape. Nominal accuracy for review is 90%. The two stress conditions use either 72% review accuracy or add the ballot-wide report flip. Every method keeps the model fitted at 76% independent project-report accuracy and 90% independent review accuracy. Stress conditions use the same nominal report draw. Report grouped by source and tape, without row-level confidence intervals.
- Spend exactly 25% of all ballot/project opportunities, rounded down. Count at most one review of any ballot/project in reuse policies. The floor is a 24% minimum share of those opportunities per source, rounded up. The same number of paid review questions is available to the legacy policy, whose independent answers and decisions stay scoped to each selected ballot/triplet (multiple paid requests may target the same ballot/project). Derive legacy scores for the very same shared reports and prior. Its budget counts each paid review exactly once, regardless of overlap.
- Compare (1) global legacy triplet ranking with query chosen by joint single-triplet gain, (2) unique global one-shot ranking by the sum of single-review gains across all affected triplets, (3) unique global sequential true marginal-gain greedy, (4) marginal-gain greedy with the same source floor, (5) unique global allocation by local subset enumeration and a concavified allocation certificate, (6) the same allocator with proportional Hamilton quotas and (7) the same allocator with the floor. Marginal greedy recomputes all seven next projected gains after each selection, including cross-triplet interactions, and does not see review answers. Its floor reserves each source's required marginal-greedy selections, then uses one shared heap. Ties use seeded ballot keys drawn after the flip indicators, then deterministic project, triplet or cost order; all methods share the same probabilities and action rule.
- For a ballot, enumerate all 128 subsets, score the sum of Bayesian expected reductions over its 35 triplets, and keep the best subset at each cost. Compute the upper concave hull of these cost-value points. Allocate hull segment slopes in descending order, honoring per-source minimums first. If an integer cost cuts a hull segment, substitute the best genuine subset at that same ballot cost. Report the relaxation upper bound minus this feasible objective, in error points. This is an optimization certificate for predicted gain, distinct from measured replay error. Independently exhaust small enumerated allocation instances to check the bound. Give a rational counterexample showing why submodular greedy need not be exact.
- Two primary gates in the nominal condition. (A) Global reuse with the certificate must save at least 1 percentage point case-weighted expected decision error compared with legacy at this same budget. (B) Floor-constrained reuse with the certificate must raise minimum coverage against globally allocated certified reuse by at least 1 percentage point, while increasing case-weighted error by at most 0.25 points. Both outcomes also report equal-source errors and proportional comparisons. Stress tests and optimality gaps are descriptive. All tapes and sources are retained. No gate changes after results.
